How it works

A professional services operating console and acquisition diligence engine. It reads a delivery organisation's own history and answers, on every run, whether the business is profitable, whether the engagements are healthy, whether the people are used well, whether the next six months can be delivered, and what to do this week.

The mechanism

One database, one engine, seventeen readings of it

Nothing on any screen is typed in. Every figure is a query result over one dataset, computed by one engine, on one set of published definitions.

That constraint is the product. A services business usually has its utilisation in one report, its margin in another and its project health in a third, each on a slightly different definition, and the first time two of them disagree in a meeting all three stop being used. Here a number appears once, with its formula, its numerator, its denominator and the vendor formula it diverges from stated beside it.

The pipeline

Eleven stages, in this order

The order is enforced, not conventional: each stage reads what the ones above it wrote, so the chain fails loudly rather than quietly producing a screen built on a stale run.

01 Schema Eight SQL files build the model: customers, people, engagements, contracts, plans, timesheets, financials, pipeline, audit.
02 Load the company Either the demonstration book, or a real one through the eight intake templates and load_target.py. This is the only stage that writes source data.
03 Intelligence engine Peer cohorts, margin prediction, risk scoring, capacity model, forecast accuracy, red flags, the weekly brief.
04 Scenarios What-if levers and deal go/no-go, run against the engine's own baseline rather than a spreadsheet.
05 Diligence pack Satisfaction and verbatims, phase durations and why they slip, hypercare, realised rate, performance cycles, product backlog, the red and amber register.
06 Earned value Cost and schedule indices per live engagement, and the quadrant each one sits in.
07 Delivery checks 56 rules over every record in scope, each with a population so a rate can be read rather than a count.
08 Resource requirement Demand against supply per product line, with hires and attrition backfill kept apart.
09 Delivery economics Billing against revenue recognition, and the economics of each commercial model.
10 Automation and AI Where a tool would pay: a measured baseline per process, the rates applied to it held as named assumptions, and the modelled saving kept separate from both.
11 Verify, then publish 142 assertions over the finished data. If one fails, nothing publishes.
What it computes

Four questions, and the honest version of each

Is the money real

Margin on recognised revenue rather than invoiced, so it does not move when an invoice is raised. Billing and recognition are reported as two signed positions, never netted: work delivered and not billed is somebody's to collect, work billed and not delivered is somebody's to deliver.

Profitability · Billing vs rev rec · T&M vs fixed fee

Is the delivery real

Progress is earned plan, not hours spent over estimate. That single choice is what lets the cost index fall below 1.00 at all, and it is why an engagement can read over cost here while a conventional PSA report shows it healthy.

Project intelligence · Earned value

Are the people used well

Utilisation is paired with realised rate, because a team can lift utilisation and lose money. Demand and supply are matched per product line at the person-week grain, and the verdict separates hiring from backfilling attrition.

Where a tool would pay

Ten automation and AI opportunities, each as three separable parts: a baseline measured from the timesheet categories the team itself uses, rates that are assumptions and are labelled as such, and the product of the two. The model can say no, and on this book it says no to three.

Automation & AI

Can it be trusted

Each of the 56 rules records what it asserts, why that matters, what fixes it, and whether the source platform blocks the condition, reports it, or is silent on it. The silent ones are the point: those are the conditions nobody finds without reconciling a spreadsheet.

Guarantees

Five things the build enforces

Each is asserted at stage 10 and fails the build, so it cannot quietly stop being true.

✓ No AI change without confirmation. Nothing predictive writes to financial or project data. The engine's health score sits beside the project manager's, and the divergence is the finding.
✓ No hard-coded values. Targets, thresholds and rates are configuration and are shown with the figures they judge.
✓ Every important change is auditable. Who, what, the previous value, the new value, and when.
✓ An override needs a reason. A project manager can overrule the engine; the comment is mandatory and is kept with the override.
✓ No AI step without a stated guardrail. Every AI-typed automation opportunity names what stays with a person, and its saving is taken net of the time that review costs.
✓ No rule can fail more records than it examined. A check whose failures exceed its population is counting two grains and calling them one, and it breaks the build rather than shipping.

Scope

What it is not

  • Not a PSA system. It does not run delivery, approve time or raise invoices. It reads what a PSA holds and answers questions that product does not.
  • Not live. Each run is an as-of snapshot, so two runs are comparable and a figure can be reproduced.
  • Not a substitute for the target's own numbers. Where the intake cannot supply something, the console says so on the screen that needs it rather than filling the gap.
  • Not multi-user. One reader, no login, no server, no write-back from the screen. SQLite rather than Postgres. It is a reporting and diligence surface, not a system of record.
  • The demonstration book is generated. It is internally consistent and deliberately contains the problems the engine exists to find, but no figure in it should be quoted.
